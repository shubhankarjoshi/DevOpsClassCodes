Patient Education

Understanding LASIK

LASIK (Laser-Assisted In Situ Keratomileusis) is one of the most widely performed procedures worldwide for correcting short-sightedness (myopia), long-sightedness (hyperopia) and astigmatism.

LASIK works by reshaping the cornea — the clear front surface of the eye — so that light entering the eye focuses correctly on the retina. This reduces or removes the need for glasses or contact lenses for many people with refractive errors.

This page is provided for general patient education. Our doctors can assess your eyes during a comprehensive eye examination and advise whether LASIK, glasses, contact lenses or another option best suits your vision needs.

How It Works

The LASIK Procedure

Before Surgery

  • Comprehensive eye examination to check refractive error, corneal thickness and overall eye health
  • Corneal mapping to create a personalised treatment plan
  • Eyes should have a stable prescription for at least 12 months
  • Conditions such as keratoconus, severe dry eye, thin cornea, uncontrolled diabetes or active eye infection may rule out LASIK

On the Day

  • Numbing eye drops are used — the procedure itself is typically painless
  • A thin corneal flap is created and gently lifted
  • A laser reshapes the underlying corneal tissue in under a minute per eye
  • The flap is repositioned — no stitches are needed
  • The full procedure usually takes about 15–20 minutes for both eyes
After LASIK

Recovery & What to Expect

First 24–48 Hours

Mild grittiness, light sensitivity or watering is common. Vision usually starts improving within a day.

Follow-up Visits

Final results stabilise over a few weeks, with scheduled visits to monitor healing.

Precautions

Avoid rubbing your eyes, swimming and strenuous exercise for the period your doctor advises.

Things to Know

LASIK does not prevent age-related presbyopia (needing reading glasses after around 40). A few patients notice temporary dry eyes, glare or halos at night. Not everyone is a candidate — a detailed evaluation is essential.
