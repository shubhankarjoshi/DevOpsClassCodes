Diabetes & Your Eyes

Why Diabetes Needs Regular Eye Checks

Diabetes can damage the tiny blood vessels inside the retina — the light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye — a condition called diabetic retinopathy.

Diabetic retinopathy is one of the leading causes of preventable vision loss. In its early stages it often causes no symptoms at all, which is why regular screening — not waiting for noticeable vision changes — is the most effective way to catch it before it threatens your sight.

Progression

Stages of Diabetic Retinopathy

Non-Proliferative (NPDR)

  • Mild: Small balloon-like swellings (microaneurysms) appear in the retina's blood vessels
  • Moderate: Blood vessels that nourish the retina become blocked
  • Severe: More vessels are blocked, depriving areas of the retina of blood supply

Proliferative (PDR)

  • The advanced stage, where the retina grows new, fragile blood vessels
  • These vessels can bleed into the eye or pull on the retina, risking retinal detachment
  • Diabetic Macular Edema — fluid build-up in the central retina — can occur at any stage and is a major cause of vision loss
What to Watch For

Symptoms & Risk Factors

Symptoms

  • Often none in the early stages
  • Blurred or fluctuating vision
  • Dark spots or floaters
  • Difficulty seeing at night
  • Colours appearing faded
  • Sudden vision loss (advanced stage)

Risk Factors

  • Longer duration of diabetes
  • Poorly controlled blood sugar
  • High blood pressure or cholesterol
  • Pregnancy
  • Smoking

Screening & Treatment

We diagnose diabetic eye disease using Fundus Photography, Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT) and Anterior Segment Imaging, and treat it with Intravitreal Injections and retinal laser (ND-YAG & Green Laser) — all available at our clinic.

Recommended Screening

All diabetics should have a full dilated eye examination at least once a year — more frequently once retinopathy is detected. Good blood sugar, blood pressure and cholesterol control significantly reduces your risk.
